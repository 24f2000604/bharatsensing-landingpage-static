Land that is not
identified cannot
be recovered.

Satellite imagery and AI assessment at the level of the land record itself — the individual parcel — across whole regions at once, in a form precise enough to act on.

The differentiator

Assessment at the unit the record already uses

Most imagery analysis produces a polygon somebody then has to argue about. Working at parcel level means every finding arrives attached to the exact unit the organisation already administers, with its own identifier. It drops into the existing workflow instead of starting a mapping exercise, and it separates two questions that are usually tangled together: whether a parcel is occupied, and whether that occupation is authorised.

The problem

Publicly held land gets built on over decades. The land record does not reflect what is actually standing there, and finding out has always meant sending people to look — a surveyor walks the parcels, compares the ground against the record, and reports back.

Across a large holding made up of many thousands of individual parcels that is slow, uneven, and effectively impossible to repeat often enough to stay current.

What we built

  • Parcel-accurate mapping. Every boundary from the land record is mapped onto current high-resolution imagery, so the unit of analysis is the same unit the owner already administers
  • AI structure detection. Each parcel is examined for permanent structures; those standing on land recorded as public are flagged
  • Report generation at scale. Print-ready reports per site, consolidated per region, in the land units already in use
  • Tracking dashboard. Records, against every flagged parcel, whether the land is under lease or auction and to whom — so occupation and authorisation are held as two separate facts

The unit of analysis is the parcel the owner already administers, so a finding arrives with an identifier that drops straight into the existing workflow rather than starting a mapping exercise.

How findings hold up

Indicative by design,
and honest about it.

Accuracy is measured, not claimed

A sample of parcels is re-checked image by image, by hand, and the measured accuracy is published alongside the result rather than estimated after the fact.

Errors skew conservative

Where the system is wrong it tends to miss rather than over-report — the safer direction for a finding that leads to action against an occupant.

Occupation is not the same as encroachment

A parcel under lease is occupied legitimately. A parcel with a structure and no lease is something else. Both facts are held against the same record so the distinction survives.

Delivered in your own format

Output is a bound, print-ready report per region alongside the digital dashboard, in the land units and record structure already in use.

Repeatable as imagery refreshes

The assessment is not a one-off exercise. It re-runs against new imagery, so the picture stays current and change over time becomes visible in itself.

Built to direct verification, not replace it

Every flagged parcel is intended for ground-level verification by the responsible team before any action is taken. The assessment prioritises where to send people.
