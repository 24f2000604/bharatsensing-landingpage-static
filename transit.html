The hazard is rarely one
thing. It is three, in
the same place.

Operational safety intelligence for transit corridors. LiDAR, imagery, incident history and inspection records read together to detect right-of-way hazards, score corridor risk, and give safety teams something defensible to act on.

The differentiator

Correlated hazard detection

A missing sign is minor. A poor turning radius is minor. An encroaching tree is minor. All three on the same approach is a serious accident waiting for its moment. Correlated hazard detection surfaces the zones where several individually tolerable defects combine into a high-risk interaction — the pattern a schedule-driven inspection almost never catches, because each defect is logged separately and closed separately.

The problem

Transit safety data arrives from everywhere and settles nowhere. Incident logs sit in one system, inspections in another, asset records in a third, and the GIS layer in a fourth. No single view tells an agency which stretch of which route is actually most dangerous.

So prioritisation falls back on what was reported loudest or most recently, and the evidence trail behind a safety decision is assembled by hand after the fact — if it can be assembled at all.

Precision-fit safety use cases

  • Right-of-way hazard detection — obstructions, debris and encroachments identified across corridors using LiDAR and imagery, for intervention before risks escalate
  • Clearance and overhead risk monitoring — continuous checks on overhead and clearance conditions that threaten vehicle movement, infrastructure or passenger safety
  • Vegetation and seasonal readiness — growth and tree-fall risk assessed with seasonal context, supporting preventive trimming and storm preparedness
  • Platform, station and pedestrian hotspots — conflict zones surfaced around platforms, access points and pedestrian interfaces from incident patterns and spatial analysis
  • Trackside and corridor asset compliance — asset condition monitored along tracks and corridors to support compliance reporting and defensible safety assessments
  • Post-event rapid assessment — corridor impact assessed quickly after storms, floods or incidents, using fresh data to prioritise inspections, closures and recovery

Severity ratings are assigned per defect, so each of these would clear its own inspection on its own ticket. Correlation is what turns three tolerable findings on one approach into the segment that gets attention first.

The intelligence

Predictive safety
for transit operations.

LiDAR and imagery safety analytics

Automatically detects clearance risks, obstructions, vegetation encroachment, right-of-way hazards and platform risks using corridor-scale LiDAR and visual data.

Corridor risk scoring and incident intelligence

Combines segment-level risk scoring with incident clustering to surface hotspots, recurring events and near-miss patterns driven by history, assets, environment and geometry.

Field and work order loop

Routes prioritized safety actions into field workflows, tracks closures and remediation, and verifies resolution with spatial and visual evidence.

Asset record accuracy

Reconciles asset inventories against on-ground reality, so the register the agency reports from reflects what is actually in the corridor.

Audit-ready safety evidence

Detected hazards, inspections, work orders and verified fixes are linked into a single traceable trail, so a safety decision can be defended with its evidence attached.

Executive safety view

Dashboards and generated narrative summaries that communicate corridor risk, trends and actions to leadership and boards without a manual reporting cycle.
