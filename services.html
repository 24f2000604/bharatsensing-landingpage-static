The layers

One record.
Three ways of seeing it.

Each layer writes to the same record, so a field observation, a drone pass and a satellite read can be set against one another instead of living in three disconnected reports. Where they disagree, the disagreement is the finding.

LAYER 01

On-ground capture

Field teams record what is actually standing, on a mobile app built for low connectivity and low trust. Capture is live-camera only — no gallery uploads — inside a geo-fence around the asset, with every photograph geo-tagged and tied to a named user who signed in by one-time password.

OTP-only sign-inLive capture, geo-fenced Two-team cross-validationAccess scoped to own area
LAYER 02

Drone and aerial survey

Where ground photographs cannot show the whole asset, we fly it. LiDAR, thermal and RGB capture produce an orthomosaic, a point cloud and an elevation model — a measurable record of the full site that can be audited from a desk, and that does not depend on what the field reported.

LiDAR + thermal + RGBPoint cloud and 3D mesh Measurable area and clearanceRepeatable per cycle
LAYER 03

Satellite assessment

The widest and cheapest layer, and the only one that scales to every asset at once. High-resolution imagery is read against the asset boundary to confirm existence, extent and change over time, covering the long tail that drones and field visits will never reach.

Whole-portfolio coverageChange detection over time Refreshes with imageryNo field mobilisation
Engagement

How a programme
actually starts.

No service begins with a platform rollout. It begins with one corridor, one region or one cohort of sites, sized so the first output arrives while the question is still live.

Scope one slice

We pick a corridor, a region or a cohort of sites with you — small enough to deliver quickly, representative enough that the result generalises.

Capture what exists

Your existing imagery, inspection records and asset register come in first. We fly or task satellite capture only for what is genuinely missing.

Return a decision

The first output is a ranked list of what to act on, in your own units and codes — not a platform login and a tutorial.

Then widen it

Once the slice is trusted, the same pipeline extends across the portfolio and settles into a refresh cadence that matches how fast the asset actually changes.

Questions

The things everyone
asks first.

Do we need our own drones or imagery?

No. We work with whatever already exists — your inspection photographs, an old LiDAR run, a GIS export — and commission or fly capture only for the gaps. Most programmes start with a surprising amount of usable data already sitting in the organisation.

What if our records are messy or in a legacy format?

That is the normal case rather than the exception. Reconciling an asset register against the authoritative source — resolving duplicates, merged rows, spelling drift and vernacular records — is part of the work before any monitoring begins, not a prerequisite you have to meet first.

Does this replace our ERP, CMMS or GIS?

No. Those stay the execution layer. We add a decision layer above them and push structured, prioritized entries back down into the systems your teams already open every morning.

Where does the data live?

Wherever your governance requires. Cloud, hybrid and fully private deployments are all supported, including environments where nothing may leave the premises.

How accurate is it, and how would we know?

Accuracy is measured by manual re-checking of a sample, and published alongside the result rather than estimated afterwards. Where a model is wrong we prefer it to miss rather than over-report, because a false flag costs someone a site visit and an argument.

Is the output a dashboard or a report?

Both, and neither is the point. The output is a ranked list of what to act on, delivered in the format your organisation already works in — print-ready where approvals need paper, exportable at every level on screen, and routed into the maintenance system where work actually gets scheduled.

How long until the first useful output?

That depends on capture, not on software. Where usable data already exists, the first ranked output typically comes out of the initial slice rather than at the end of a rollout.
