Telecom plans need a
living view of physical
reality.

A continuously refreshed digital twin of every tower site, built from LiDAR, drone and satellite capture. Validate upgrades, check clearances and line of sight, and rank structural risk — without sending anyone to the tower to find out.

The differentiator

Remote validation against a live twin

Most site visits exist to answer a question an accurate twin could have answered from a desk: will this antenna fit, is there clearance, what is actually mounted up there, is the structure sound enough to carry more. A twin that refreshes rather than ages turns a periodic survey into an operating workflow, and turns a truck roll into a query.

The problem

Fragmented site data leaves operators without a trusted view of the network. There is no unified physical picture across thousands of sites, mounted equipment and structural safety are uncertain until someone climbs, and RF plans are drawn against assumptions rather than against real obstructions.

The consequences are concrete. Upgrade planning relies on records that do not reflect the as-built site. Validation requires repeat visits. Asset records drift tower by tower, and nobody can say how far.

What the twin answers

  • The current physical configuration of each tower and site — structural elements, mounted equipment, antenna orientation, clearances and obstructions
  • Whether a planned upgrade actually fits, before anyone is dispatched
  • How real-world obstructions compare with the theoretical RF plan in dense environments
  • Where corrosion, tilt, deformation or unauthorized equipment have appeared against the baseline scan
  • Which sites breach zoning rules, height restrictions or municipal constraints
  • Which interventions matter most, ranked by safety and regulatory exposure

Capture positions are planned so the structure is covered from every angle needed for mesh generation. The twin that comes out answers the questions that otherwise send a crew to the tower.

Use cases

Planning through
maintenance.

Six use cases on one twin. The first three decide what to build; the last three keep it standing.

Tower digital twin

Structural elements, mounted equipment, antenna orientation, clearances and obstructions — establishing the current physical configuration of each tower and site.

Rollout and upgrade planning

Antenna placement, line of sight, height and clearance constraints simulated and validated remotely before deployment is scheduled.

Coverage and line-of-sight validation

Real-world constraints compared against theoretical RF plans, so design intent is checked against physical reality in dense environments.

Structural health and compliance

Corrosion, tilt, deformation and unauthorized equipment detected against baseline scans, with interventions ranked by safety and regulatory exposure.

Zoning and permit intelligence

Zoning rules, height restrictions and municipal constraints overlaid on the twin, flagging violations early enough to support approvals.

Automated operations and maintenance

Detected issues translated into prioritized work orders in ERP and CMMS, so dispatch follows condition rather than the calendar.
